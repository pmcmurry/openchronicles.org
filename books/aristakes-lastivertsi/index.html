About this work

Aristakes Lastivertsi wrote in the generation that watched the Armenian kingdoms pass to Byzantium and then fall to the Seljuk Turks. He is a contemporary witness: he saw the consequences of the annexation of Ani, he records the sack of Arcn in 1049 at length, and he carries the account through to the capture of the emperor Romanos Diogenes in 1071. He also wrote the fullest surviving account of the Tondrakian movement.

He is not a detached observer. He believes the catastrophe is punishment for sin, he says so repeatedly, and his invective against Greeks, Turks, courtiers, and heretics is unsparing. None of that is softened here. His dates, his numbers, his errors, and his anger stand as he wrote them, and where his text is genuinely uncertain the notes say so rather than guess.

Review status. This edition has not undergone independent Armenian-language review, philological review, or external scholarly peer review, and should not be cited as though it had. It rests on a documented source-first process: every unit verified character by character against the 1963 page images before drafting, production QA, a blind independent audit, comparator collation with no rendering adopted, a whole-work terminology audit, and a final Armenian-to-English coverage audit over printed pp. 21–145. Readers needing certainty on a particular reading should consult the 1963 edition itself.

Translated from the Classical Armenian with AI assistance, edited by Paul McMurry

Translated from Classical Armenian verified character by character against 300 dpi page images of the Yuzbashyan 1963 critical edition before any English was drafted, with AI assistance; edited and verified by Paul McMurry. Scripture is translated from Aristakes' own wording and is never replaced with the wording of an English Bible: where he quotes loosely, conflates, or misremembers, the English follows him and the divergence is disclosed. The author's dating, his numbers, his errors and his invective all stand as he wrote them. Square brackets mark the source editor's supplements, not the translator's. Guillemets « » mark a quotation the 1963 edition itself sets in guillemets; straight quotes " " mark direct speech the edition leaves typographically unmarked and the Armenian marks lexically instead. The reading page carries no note cues; the 498 back-of-book notes are keyed by chapter and lemma.

Produced from the 1963 page images without using any existing English translation as a source text. The corpus translation standard bars consulting an existing translation at any stage, permitting comparison only after freeze; this project's brief instead required a post-draft, pre-freeze collation against R. Bedrosian's English version, unit by unit, and that departure is authorized and disclosed. It was narrowed by procedure: the comparator was opened only after a unit's draft was locked and its independent audit saved, every divergence was resolved from the Armenian page image alone, and no rendering was adopted from it anywhere in this work. Two distinctive phrases that converged by accident were re-drafted away.

Limitations

This edition has NOT undergone independent Armenian-language review, philological review, or external scholarly peer review, and it must not be represented as having done so. The project-owner sign-off of 13 September 2026 accepts the machine-validated text and its editorial presentation; it certifies no Armenian reading. Correctness rests on the documented source-first workflow: verification against page images before drafting, production QA, a blind independent audit, post-draft comparator collation with nothing adopted, a whole-work terminology audit, and a final Armenian-to-English reverse-coverage audit over printed pp. 21–145 certifying 3,048 ledger rows with none left unresolved. Open textual cruxes are disclosed in the notes rather than resolved by guess; twelve are named there. The scripture notes are keyed by printed page, line and Armenian incipit rather than by an English lemma, because the citation table carries no English column. One Armenian word at p. 115 l. 6 is left unrepresented in the English rather than forced. Chapters 1–11 are British-spelled and Chapters 12–25 American, an unresolved seam at the chapter 11/12 boundary. Printed p. 146 onward is the 1963 editor's bibliography and indices and lies outside this translation.
