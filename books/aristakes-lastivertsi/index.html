About this work

Aristakes Lastivertsi wrote in the generation that watched the Armenian kingdoms pass to Byzantium and then fall to the Seljuk Turks. He is a contemporary witness: he saw the consequences of the annexation of Ani, he records the sack of Arcn in 1049 at length, and he carries the account through to the capture of the emperor Romanos Diogenes in 1071. He also wrote the fullest surviving account of the Tondrakian movement.

He is not a detached observer. He believes the catastrophe is punishment for sin, he says so repeatedly, and his invective against Greeks, Turks, courtiers, and heretics is unsparing. None of that is softened in either edition. His dates, his numbers, his errors, and his anger stand as he wrote them, and where his text is genuinely uncertain the source-faithful notes say so rather than guess.

Which edition

The source-faithful translation is the record: it is the edition to cite, and the one that carries the textual notes. The reader's edition is the same history as a book to read through — adapted from the source-faithful translation alone, not from the Armenian, with syntax and paragraphing revised and repeated formula condensed. Every one of the source-faithful edition's 170 paragraphs is represented in it, and a paragraph map records where. It runs about 5% shorter than the source-faithful text.

Review status. Neither edition has undergone independent Armenian-language review, philological review, or external scholarly peer review, and neither should be cited as though it had. The translation rests on a documented source-first process: every unit verified character by character against the 1963 page images before drafting, production QA, a blind independent audit, comparator collation with no rendering adopted, a whole-work terminology audit, and a final Armenian-to-English coverage audit over printed pp. 21–145. Readers needing certainty on a particular reading should consult the 1963 edition itself.

Limitations

This edition has NOT undergone independent Armenian-language review, philological review, or external scholarly peer review, and it must not be represented as having done so. The project-owner sign-off of 13 September 2026 accepts the machine-validated text and its editorial presentation; it certifies no Armenian reading. Correctness rests on the documented source-first workflow: verification against page images before drafting, production QA, a blind independent audit, post-draft comparator collation with nothing adopted, a whole-work terminology audit, and a final Armenian-to-English reverse-coverage audit over printed pp. 21–145 certifying 3,048 ledger rows with none left unresolved. Open textual cruxes are disclosed in the notes rather than resolved by guess; twelve are named there. The scripture notes are keyed by printed page, line and Armenian incipit rather than by an English lemma, because the citation table carries no English column. One Armenian word at p. 115 l. 6 is left unrepresented in the English rather than forced. Chapters 1–11 are British-spelled and Chapters 12–25 American, an unresolved seam at the chapter 11/12 boundary. Printed p. 146 onward is the 1963 editor's bibliography and indices and lies outside this translation.

Limitations

Neither this edition nor the source-faithful translation it derives from has undergone independent Armenian-language review, philological review, or external scholarly peer review, and neither should be cited as though it had. This is a derived narrative edition, not a second independent translation from the Classical Armenian; prefer the source-faithful translation for citation of exact wording, for the textual cruxes, and for the notes, none of which are carried here. Completeness rather than length is the integrity claim: all 26 units and all 170 running paragraphs of the frozen source-faithful edition are represented across 175 reader paragraphs, with 0 episodes cut, 0 paragraphs lost and 0 paragraphs invented, and a paragraph-level map traces every paragraph in both directions. On length, this reader's edition is about 5% shorter than the source-faithful text (41,669 running words to 39,579), against the 10–20% the Open Chronicles standard sets for the form. The shortfall is recorded rather than closed by cutting: the source-faithful translation was produced by a process that removed padding before adaptation began, so less was available to condense than the guideline assumes, and reaching the figure would have required cutting episodes or compressing the author's argument. Neither is permitted. Two presentation differences from the frozen base are disclosed and touch no substance: about 47 reverential capitals on divine pronouns are lowercased, the frozen edition having capitalised them inconsistently; and one terminal full stop sits outside its guillemets at Chapter 18, the only one of the work's 149 quoted spans not carried character for character. Chapter numerals and headings are transmitted source text and are kept rather than replaced with modern scene titles; the closing Discourse of Commemoration is unnumbered in the source and unnumbered here, and there is no twenty-sixth chapter.
